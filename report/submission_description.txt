This report presents an exploratory data analysis (EDA) of the public nycflights13 dataset, which contains all 336,776 domestic flights that departed New York City's three airports (JFK, LaGuardia and Newark) in 2013, sourced from the US Bureau of Transportation Statistics and joined with NOAA hourly weather data. I chose it because flight punctuality closely mirrors the on-time delivery (OTIF) KPIs I work with as an operations analyst, and because it contains real data-quality issues across several linked tables.

All work was done in R (dplyr, tidyr, ggplot2) using three reproducible scripts for cleaning, analysis and visualization. During cleaning I found that missing departure and arrival times were not random: they identified 8,255 cancelled and 1,175 diverted flights, so I flagged them instead of imputing. I also corrected an impossible 1,048 mph wind reading, dropped two mostly empty columns, converted HHMM clock times, created calendar and on-time features, and validated the weather and aircraft joins.

The exploratory analysis covers summary statistics, skewness, group comparisons by carrier, airport, month, hour and weekday, correlation and regression, and anomaly detection using z-scores and the IQR rule. Nine visualizations of seven types (histogram, box plot, line chart, time series, heat map, scatter plot, correlation matrix and bar charts) are each explained with code and interpretation.

Key findings: delays are heavily right-skewed; they build steadily through the day; arrival delay is almost fully inherited from departure delay (R-squared 0.84); summer and December are the worst periods; the February 2013 blizzard caused over 50% cancellations; and rain reduces the on-time rate from 77% to 51%. The report closes with limitations and next steps, including a predictive delay model.
